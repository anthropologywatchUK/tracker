Why Track Anthropology Programmes?

Anthropology programmes across the United Kingdom are facing unprecedented pressures. Financial deficits, declining enrolments in humanities and social sciences, institutional restructuring, and shifts in government higher education policy are combining to reshape the disciplinary landscape. Departments that have operated for decades are being merged, reduced, or closed entirely.

UK Anthropology Watch provides a centralised, transparent record of these institutional changes. Modelled on the American Anthropological Association's Anthropology Watch tracker, this project aims to support informed public discussion, enable comparative analysis across institutions, and preserve an accurate historical record of what is happening to anthropology in UK higher education.

In September 2025, the British Academy's Cold Spots report found that anthropology is among the SHAPE subjects showing significant decline in provision, with large geographic gaps emerging across Scotland, northern England, the East Midlands, and Wales. The discipline's future depends on clear-eyed documentation of these trends.

Methodology & Status Definitions

This tracker documents publicly reported changes to anthropology programmes at UK higher education institutions. Status categories are defined as follows:

  • Active — The programme is recruiting students and operating normally.
  • Monitoring — The programme is active but faces institutional pressures, limited provision, or is in a university under financial strain.
  • Under pressure — Publicly reported financial pressures, voluntary redundancies, or restructuring affecting the department, but programme continues.
  • Staff reductions — Confirmed staff cuts (voluntary or compulsory) that have reduced departmental capacity.
  • Admissions paused — New student admissions have been temporarily or permanently suspended.
  • Closed — The programme has been permanently discontinued or the department dissolved.

Data sources include institutional announcements, Times Higher Education, UCU reports, UCAS listings, the Complete University Guide, the British Academy SHAPE Observatory, and the ASA's Condition of UK Anthropology (2023) report.
